\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}


Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) enables wireless networks to reuse
spectrum, hardware, and signal-processing resources for both data transmission
and environmental sensing~\cite{liu_dual_functional_6g,
	gonzalez_revolution_2024}. UAV-enabled ISAC extends this capability to aerial
sensing scenarios, where platform mobility can improve coverage, line-of-sight
availability, and sensing geometry~\cite{mu_uav_isac,
	meng_uav_isac_challenges}. When multiple UAVs cooperate, the same target can
be observed from different bistatic geometries, providing complementary spatial
information for more reliable sensing decisions~\cite{meng_cooperative_isac_2025}.

In such a distributed network, however, inter-UAV reporting links have limited
rate and reliability, so more cooperative observations do not necessarily lead
to higher fusion gain~\cite{meng_cooperative_isac_2025,
	chen_node_selection_backhaul_2025}. Each observation is generated by a
specific transmitter--receiver--target geometry, reported over an inter-UAV
link, and then fused with the others, and its contribution can be weakened at
any stage of this sensing--reporting--fusion process. A geometrically favorable
return may suffer from inefficient delay-Doppler (DD)-domain energy
capture~\cite{yuan_dd_isac, keskin_mimo_otfs_isac}; its soft statistic may be
distorted by unreliable reporting~\cite{chawla_massive_mimo_dd,
	chawla_parameter_detection_mimo_wsn}; or its reporting cost may be too large
relative to its marginal fusion gain~\cite{meng_network_level_isac_2024,
	chen_node_selection_backhaul_2025}. As a result, all-neighbor fusion or
sensing-SINR-based activation can be inefficient when inter-UAV communication
resources are limited.

Existing studies have examined these aspects, though largely from separate
viewpoints. Aerial sensing architectures, mobility, and resource management are
the main focus of UAV-ISAC work~\cite{mu_uav_isac, meng_uav_isac_challenges}.
Sensing--communication tradeoffs under resource or backhaul constraints, by
contrast, have been considered in network-level ISAC and cooperative sensing
studies~\cite{meng_network_level_isac_2024,
	chen_node_selection_backhaul_2025}. On the detection side, imperfect channel
knowledge and unreliable delivery have been shown to degrade detection
performance~\cite{chawla_massive_mimo_dd, chawla_parameter_detection_mimo_wsn}.
Separately, OTFS-based ISAC exploits DD-domain structures for high-mobility
sensing and communication~\cite{yuan_dd_isac, keskin_mimo_otfs_isac}. Each line
captures one facet of the problem, yet communication-constrained
triplet-level scheduling that couples these facets for multi-UAV bistatic
OTFS-ISAC has received limited attention.

This gap motivates a triplet-level scheduling perspective. Node-selection and
resource-allocation studies act at the level of UAV nodes or
links~\cite{chen_node_selection_backhaul_2025}, and distributed-detection
studies model reporting reliability without the underlying bistatic
geometry~\cite{chawla_massive_mimo_dd, chawla_parameter_detection_mimo_wsn};
neither, however, exposes the unit that actually carries a cooperative
observation. In multi-UAV bistatic sensing that unit is a target-dependent
transmitter--receiver--target triplet, whose value depends jointly on its
bistatic sensing contribution, the reliability and delay of its reporting link,
and the DD-domain energy-capture efficiency of the corresponding OTFS
observation~\cite{yuan_dd_isac}. Judging these factors in isolation is
misleading---a triplet with high nominal sensing-SINR can still add little after
fusion if its soft statistic is reported over a slow or unreliable channel, or
if its DD-domain energy is poorly captured. We therefore treat each triplet as a
schedulable soft-information reporting unit and score it on a calibrated sensing
contribution, reporting reliability, exchange delay, and DD-domain efficiency
taken jointly, aiming to select a compact set of cooperative observations that
preserves most of the sensing gain while reducing signaling overhead and
reporting delay.

Our main contributions are as follows.

\begin{itemize}
	\item We formulate communication-constrained multi-UAV OTFS-ISAC cooperative
	sensing as a triplet-level soft-information link-selection problem. Each
	candidate is characterized by its bistatic sensing contribution, reporting
	reliability, exchange delay, and DD-domain energy-capture efficiency.
	
	\item We develop a communication-error-calibrated marginal-deflection metric
	and a C2F DD-aware Lagrangian link-selection method. The method evaluates
	target-dependent candidates by balancing marginal fusion gain and reporting
	delay, while applying local DD-grid refinement only to shortlisted links.
	
	\item Simulation results in a 15-UAV, 10-target aerial sensing scenario show
	that the proposed method retains most of the all-neighbor detection gain with
	substantially lower signaling overhead and reporting delay. It also provides
	a more favorable detection--delay tradeoff than sensing-SINR-based selection.
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:system_model} presents the multi-UAV OTFS-ISAC system model.

concludes the paper.
