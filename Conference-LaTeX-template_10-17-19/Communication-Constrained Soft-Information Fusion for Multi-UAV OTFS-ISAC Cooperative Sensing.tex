	\begin{abstract}
		Communication-constrained cooperative sensing depends on both the selected
		observations and the locations where their soft information is fused. We
		propose target-local fusion for prior-assisted multi-UAV OTFS-ISAC sensing.
		Each target is assigned to the UAV nearest its predicted position, after
		which a detector-aligned adaptive coarse-to-fine (C2F) selector chooses local
		observations and finite-blocklength remote reports. In 1000 paired trials with
		15 UAVs and 10 targets, the proposed method achieved $P_D=0.9764$ and a
		worst-target $P_D$ of 0.966. Its detection difference from sensing-SINR
		selection was not statistically significant, while it reduced the mean payload and serial
		reporting delay by 83.7\%. A fusion-rule ablation showed that this saving
		primarily arose because target-local planning retained more evidence at its
		fusion UAV. The result is limited to prior-assisted sensing with the stated
		finite-blocklength and serial-reporting model; performance decreased under
		large prediction errors and sparse deployments.
	\end{abstract}
