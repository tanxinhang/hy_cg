\subsection{Protocol and Nominal Configuration}

We evaluate prior-assisted confirmation in a $4000\,\mathrm{m}\times
4000\,\mathrm{m}$ region with $M=15$ UAVs and $Q=10$ targets. Table~
\ref{tab:simulation_parameters} lists the released V1 configuration. All main
methods use the same paired geometries, detector randomness, physical model,
and observation caps. The confirmatory comparison uses 1000
Monte-Carlo trials. Fusion and operating-envelope sweeps use 100 trials per
condition with seed 2026 and are therefore diagnostic rather than substitutes
for the main evidence.

\subsection{Detection Performance and Communication Cost}

Figure~\ref{fig:v1_main} compares target-local adaptive C2F with two strong
rules. Exact-marginal greedy maximizes each step's deflection-based utility
gain without a delay price, with total observations capped at V1's count and
six per target. ``Exact'' refers to marginal evaluation, not global optimality.
Sensing-SINR ranks coarse sensing SINR and matches V1's per-target observation
counts. Both use V1's fusion graph and feasibility checks; remote-report counts
are intentionally outcomes, not matched constraints. V1 attains
$P_D=0.9764$ (95\% CI: $[0.9732,0.9792]$), versus 0.9408 for exact-marginal
greedy and 0.9784 for Sensing-SINR. Worst-target values are 0.966, 0.931, and
0.972. The paired V1-minus-baseline intervals are $+0.0356$
($[0.0311,0.0401]$) and $-0.0020$ ($[-0.0050,0.0010]$), respectively.
Thus, V1 improved on exact-marginal selection; its detection difference from
Sensing-SINR was not statistically resolved. This does not establish
equivalence or non-inferiority. The supported headline is communication
efficiency with no detected significant change in detection.

V1 selects 12.101 observations and
sends 0.751 remote reports on average, corresponding to 0.481~kbit and
0.801~ms. Sensing-SINR selects the same 12.101 observations but sends 4.595
reports, 2.941~kbit, and 4.901~ms. V1 therefore reduces payload and serial delay
by 83.7\% without a significant $P_D$ change. Both savings follow from the
same report-count reduction under fixed packet size and serial MAC. A separate
MC=200 full-refinement control uses the same detector-PD greedy rule.
Adaptive C2F uses 61.06 versus 853.65 fine evaluations (92.85\% fewer),
with $P_D=0.9775$ versus 0.9715. The paired difference is 0.0060
($[0.0005,0.0115]$); full refinement is a computational control, not an oracle.

\subsection{Fusion-Placement Ablation}

Figure~\ref{fig:fusion_ablation} changes only the fusion destination rule. The
target-agnostic max-in-rate rule gives $P_D=0.932$, worst-target $P_D=0.900$,
6.65 remote reports, and 7.093~ms. A max-min-rate alternative gives 0.967,
0.940, 6.40, and 6.827~ms. Nearest-target fusion reaches 0.975 and 0.960 with
only 0.74 remote reports and 0.789~ms. It also reduces selected observations
from 19.89 and 15.61 to 12.24. The nearest-target rule therefore converted more
selected evidence into local observations with unit reliability and no packet
cost.

Nearest-target fusion distributes targets across 7.13 fusion UAVs on average,
versus one for the other rules. Its 0.70 conflict-graph slots indicate possible
parallelism, not achieved delay; all reported delays use serial MAC.

\subsection{Operating Boundaries}

Increasing position error from 50 to 500~m reduced $P_D$ from 0.988 to
0.906. Increasing deployment side from 2500 to 5500~m reduced it from 0.989
to 0.940 and increased reports from 0.05 to 2.67. Other 100-trial scans in
the supplement calibrate parameters without supporting universal recommendations.

\subsection{Component Validation and Runtime}

A separate audit compares the normalized DD expression with a compact OTFS
impulse-response simulation at 512 fractional offsets. The maximum local-window
energy discrepancy is $3.2\times10^{-15}$; the coarse sinc approximation differs
by at most $1.06\times10^{-3}$. Six waveform-derived LLR tests with $10^5$ draws
each give at most 0.00184 discrepancy from exact single-report mixture detection,
but up to 0.0447 from the moment-based prediction. This supports the component
law while exposing the scheduling approximation's limits, not validating a
complete multi-target radio. On 30 additional scenarios, median selector time
is 0.556~s for adaptive C2F and 2.032~s for full refinement on a Ryzen 7 7800X3D.
Timing excludes shared table construction and precomputed DD gains; it is not
end-to-end latency. The supplement supplies seeds, intervals, and limitations.
