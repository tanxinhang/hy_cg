
\section{Simulation Results}
\label{sec:simulation_results}

We evaluate the proposed communication-error-calibrated and C2F DD-aware
Lagrangian link-selection scheme in a high-mobility multi-UAV aerial sensing
scenario. Unless otherwise specified, $15$ UAVs cooperatively sense $10$ aerial
targets in a $4000~\mathrm{m}\times4000~\mathrm{m}$ area. The UAVs and targets
move with speeds of $30$--$60~\mathrm{m/s}$ and $50$--$90~\mathrm{m/s}$,
respectively. We use the all-neighbor scheme as an upper-resource reference
and set the target false-alarm probability to $P_{\mathrm{FA}}=0.05$ in all
tests. Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters} lists the main simulation
parameters.

\subsection{Performance and Tradeoff Analysis}

For the main comparison in Fig.~\ref{fig:main_comparison}, the proposed method
achieves $P_D=0.6648$, which retains about $94.8\%$ of the all-neighbor
detection probability. At the same time, it reduces signaling overhead and
exchange delay by approximately $92.1\%$ and $94.8\%$, respectively. In
absolute terms, the exchange delay decreases from about $5.1\times10^2$ ms
under all-neighbor fusion to about $27$ ms with the proposed method. This
confirms that all-neighbor fusion is a useful upper-resource reference, but its
reporting cost is too high for low-latency cooperative sensing.

A major source of this gain is the avoidance of ineffective soft-information
reporting. Sensing-SINR-based selection tends to choose links with strong
sensing responses, without explicitly checking whether their soft statistics
can be reported reliably and quickly. By combining communication-error-
calibrated marginal deflection with delay-aware Lagrangian pricing, the
proposed method keeps most useful cooperative observations and discards links
whose reporting cost is not justified by their effective fusion contribution.

Under the same per-target selected-link budget, the proposed method achieves
higher detection probability than Sensing-SINR and Top-K Sensing-SINR. Compared
with Top-K Deflection, it provides similar detection performance while reducing
exchange delay by about $29\%$. This behavior is consistent with the
delay-aware Lagrangian term, which discourages unreliable or slow
soft-information links even when their sensing or deflection scores are high.

\begin{figure}[!t]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{figs/fig3_lambda_tradeoff.pdf}
	\caption{Sensitivity to the Lagrangian communication price $\lambda_c$.}
	\label{fig:lambda_tradeoff}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!t]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{figs/fig5_comm_sweep.pdf}
	\caption{Sensitivity to the communication-rate constraint $R_{\min}$.}
	\label{fig:comm_sweep}
\end{figure}

Varying $\lambda_c$ in Fig.~\ref{fig:lambda_tradeoff} changes the selected-link
pattern directly. A larger $\lambda_c$ reduces the number of selected links,
signaling overhead, and exchange delay, with only a moderate loss in detection
probability. Hence, $\lambda_c$ serves as a practical control knob for the
detection--delay tradeoff. Changing $R_{\min}$ in
Fig.~\ref{fig:comm_sweep} affects the problem in a different way: a larger rate
requirement shrinks the feasible reporting graph and weakens the cooperative
sensing gain. In the low-to-moderate constraint region, the proposed method
still maintains a better sensing--communication tradeoff than Sensing-SINR by
prioritizing reliable and low-delay links.

Taken together, these two sensitivity tests separate the roles of algorithmic
pricing and communication feasibility. The Lagrangian price adjusts the penalty
on reporting delay, whereas the rate constraint changes which inter-UAV links
can be used for soft-information exchange. When the feasible reporting graph
becomes too sparse, all cooperative schemes degrade, indicating that
communication feasibility remains a fundamental bottleneck.

\subsection{Ablation and Robustness Analysis}

\begin{table}[!t]
	\centering
	\caption{Component ablation under the same selected-link budget}
	\label{tab:ablation_compact}
	\footnotesize
	\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2pt}
	\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
		\toprule
		Variant & $P_D$ & Bits & Delay & Worst \\
		& & (kbit) & (ms) & $P_D$ \\
		\midrule
		Full & 0.6540 & 16.26 & 26.82 & 0.631 \\
		w/o target priority & 0.6198 & 16.63 & 29.59 & 0.601 \\
		w/o delay price & 0.6564 & 16.48 & 29.21 & 0.635 \\
		w/o comm.-error calib. & 0.5856 & 13.77 & 28.48 & 0.572 \\
		w/o C2F refine & 0.6223 & 16.41 & 27.42 & 0.604 \\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For the component study in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_compact}, all variants are
evaluated under a fixed selected-link budget. The ``Full'' row is therefore
used for within-table comparison and is not intended to duplicate the
main-comparison value in Fig.~\ref{fig:main_comparison}. Among the tested
variants, removing communication-error calibration causes the largest
degradation, reducing $P_D$ from $0.6540$ to $0.5856$. This highlights the
importance of calibrating unreliable exchanged soft information before fusion.
Without C2F refinement, $P_D$ decreases to $0.6223$, which indicates that local
DD refinement helps recover fractional delay-Doppler energy leakage. Removing
target priority degrades both average and worst-target detection performance.
Without delay-aware pricing, $P_D$ and worst-target $P_D$ slightly increase,
but the exchange delay also becomes larger; this matches the role of the
delay-price term in trading a small amount of detection gain for lower
reporting latency.

In a separate DD-domain ablation under the main-comparison configuration, C2F
refinement improves $P_D$ from $0.6262$ to $0.6648$. It matches full local
refinement while reducing fine DD-grid evaluations by about $41.1\%$. This
experiment isolates the effect of DD-domain refinement and shows that local
DD-grid recovery is useful, whereas applying it to every feasible triplet is
unnecessary.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.80\textwidth]{figs/fig6_robustness.pdf}
	\caption{Robustness under communication errors and residual interference.}
	\label{fig:robustness}
\end{figure*}

Robustness is further evaluated under communication errors and residual
interference in Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}. As the soft-error scale or residual
factor increases, all methods degrade. The proposed method still maintains a
favorable detection--delay tradeoff because communication reliability and
residual-interference-aware calibration penalize unreliable links before
fusion. These results support the robustness of the proposed soft-information
fusion strategy under imperfect reporting conditions.
