
\section{Simulation Results}
\label{sec:simulation_results}

We evaluate the proposed communication-error-calibrated and C2F DD-aware
Lagrangian link-selection scheme in a high-mobility multi-UAV aerial sensing
scenario. Unless otherwise specified, $15$ UAVs cooperatively sense $10$ aerial
targets in a $4000~\mathrm{m}\times4000~\mathrm{m}$ area. The UAVs and targets
move with speeds of $30$--$60~\mathrm{m/s}$ and $50$--$90~\mathrm{m/s}$,
respectively. We use the all-neighbor scheme as an upper-resource reference
and set the target false-alarm probability to $P_{\mathrm{FA}}=0.05$ in all
tests. Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters} lists the main simulation
parameters.

\subsection{Performance and Tradeoff Analysis}

For the main comparison in Fig.~\ref{fig:main_comparison}, the proposed method
achieves $P_D=0.9481$, which retains about $97.2\%$ of the all-neighbor
detection probability. At the same time, it reduces signaling overhead and
exchange delay by approximately $96.7\%$ and $99.0\%$, respectively. In
absolute terms, the exchange delay decreases from about $6.9\times10^2$ ms
under all-neighbor fusion to about $6.9$ ms with the proposed method. This
confirms that all-neighbor fusion is a useful upper-resource reference, but its
reporting cost is too high for low-latency cooperative sensing.

A major source of this gain is the avoidance of ineffective soft-information
reporting. Sensing-SINR-based selection tends to choose links with strong
sensing responses, without explicitly checking whether their soft statistics
can be reported reliably and quickly. By combining communication-error-
calibrated marginal deflection with delay-aware Lagrangian pricing, the
proposed method keeps most useful cooperative observations and discards links
whose reporting cost is not justified by their effective fusion contribution;
it attains $0.9481$ against $0.8705$ for Sensing-SINR and $0.8148$ for
Shortest-Bistatic, a margin of $7.8$ percentage points over the strongest
competing rule.

Under the same per-target selected-link budget, the proposed method achieves
higher detection probability than every baseline except the all-neighbor upper
reference, and it marginally exceeds Top-K Deflection ($0.9481$ vs.\ $0.9470$)
while reducing exchange delay by about $6.5\%$. This behavior is consistent with
the delay-aware Lagrangian term, which discourages unreliable or slow
soft-information links even when their sensing or deflection scores are high.

	\caption{Sensitivity to the Lagrangian communication price $\lambda_c$.}

\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!t]

	\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{figs/fig5_comm_sweep.pdf}

	\label{fig:comm_sweep}
\end{figure}

Varying $\lambda_c$ in Fig.~\ref{fig:lambda_tradeoff} changes the selected-link
pattern directly. Over more than two decades of $\lambda_c$ (from
$5\times10^{-4}$ to $0.2$) the number of selected links falls from $16.5$ to
$12.6$, the exchange delay from $8.5$ to $4.3$ ms and the overhead from $10.6$
to $8.1$ kbit, while $P_D$ changes by less than half a percentage point
($0.9523$ to $0.9477$). Hence $\lambda_c$ is a practical control knob for the
detection--delay tradeoff that buys latency and overhead almost for free.
Changing $R_{\min}$ in Fig.~\ref{fig:comm_sweep} affects the problem in a
different way: a larger rate requirement shrinks the feasible reporting graph
and weakens the cooperative sensing gain. $P_D$ falls from $0.9678$ at
$R_{\min}=0.1$ to $0.4797$ at $R_{\min}=2$ (in Mbit/s). In the low-to-moderate
constraint region the proposed method still maintains a better
sensing--communication tradeoff than Sensing-SINR by prioritizing reliable and
low-delay links: at $R_{\min}=0.1$ it attains $0.9678$ against $0.8927$, and it
only converges to the baseline once the feasible graph is so sparse that almost
no selection freedom remains.

Taken together, these two sensitivity tests separate the roles of algorithmic
pricing and communication feasibility. The Lagrangian price adjusts the penalty
on reporting delay, whereas the rate constraint changes which inter-UAV links
can be used for soft-information exchange. When the feasible reporting graph
becomes too sparse, all cooperative schemes degrade, indicating that
communication feasibility remains a fundamental bottleneck. We note that under
the corrected interference model the fraction of feasible reporting edges at the
nominal $R_{\min}=0.2$ rises from $0.33$ to $0.50$, because the
scale-relative SINR guard restores the communication SINR on weakly interfered
links. The nominal operating point is therefore only moderately constrained; the
constraint becomes binding for $R_{\min}\gtrsim1$, where $P_D$ falls to $0.78$
and the selection freedom effectively disappears.

For the component study in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_compact}, all variants are
evaluated under a fixed selected-link budget. The ``Full'' row is therefore
used for within-table comparison and is not intended to duplicate the
main-comparison value in Fig.~\ref{fig:main_comparison}. Removing
communication-error calibration causes the largest degradation, reducing $P_D$
from $0.9469$ to $0.8445$, which highlights the importance of calibrating
unreliable exchanged soft information before fusion. Removing target priority
is comparably damaging ($0.8504$) and additionally inflates the overhead from
$9.17$ to $15.88$ kbit and the delay from $6.56$ to $9.40$ ms, confirming that
the priority term is what keeps the selector from over-investing in targets
that are already resolved. Without delay-aware pricing $P_D$ is marginally
higher ($0.9482$), but the exchange delay grows by about $9\%$; this matches the
role of the delay-price term in trading a small amount of detection gain for
lower reporting latency. Notably, removing the softmin weighting raises $P_D$
from $0.9469$ to $0.9540$ but costs more overhead and delay ($10.00$ kbit and
$7.18$ ms), indicating that the softmin-$\alpha$ fairness term currently lacks
experimental support; it is retained only as a configurable mechanism and its
justifying functional is left to future work.

In a separate DD-domain ablation under the main-comparison configuration, C2F
refinement improves $P_D$ from $0.9481$ to $0.9572$. It matches full local
refinement ($0.9537$, the difference being within Monte-Carlo error) while
reducing fine DD-grid evaluations by about $44.6\%$. This experiment isolates
the effect of DD-domain refinement and shows that local DD-grid recovery is
useful, whereas applying it to every feasible triplet is unnecessary.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.80\textwidth]{figs/fig6_robustness.pdf}
	\caption{Robustness along four axes: communication error model, soft-error
	scale, residual direct-interference floor (decoupled model) and direct-path
	cancellation (coupled model).}
	\label{fig:robustness}
\end{figure*}

Robustness is evaluated along four axes in Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}. Under
communication errors the proposed method attains $P_D=0.9481$ for erasure,
$0.9756$ for the biased model and $0.9050$ for bit flipping, so even the
worst case retains $92.8\%$ of the error-free value. Sweeping the soft-error
scale from $1$ to $4$ degrades $P_D$ only from $0.9488$ to $0.9446$, at the
cost of growing the exchange delay from $4.9$ to $8.3$ ms. The two
interference axes are qualitatively different: the residual-floor axis is
evaluated inside the decoupled model, where it controls the sensing
interference directly and drives $P_D$ from $0.6550$ down to $0.4970$ over two
decades, whereas the direct-path cancellation axis is the ISAC-specific axis
analysed in Section~\ref{subsec:interference_coupling}. In every case the
proposed method maintains a favorable detection--delay tradeoff because
communication reliability and interference-aware calibration penalize
unreliable links before fusion. These results support the robustness of the
proposed soft-information fusion strategy under imperfect reporting
conditions.

\subsection{Model Refinements and Theoretical Guarantees}
\label{subsec:refinements}

This subsection validates the stricter model components introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:system_model} and the structural guarantee of the greedy rule.

\emph{Strictly derived soft statistic (local LLR).} Replacing the Gaussian
abstraction \eqref{eq:gaussian_soft_stat} by the waveform-derived local LLR
\eqref{eq:local_llr}--\eqref{eq:llr_kld} removes the hand-set scale
$\kappa_\mu$. Under the LLR model the proposed method attains $P_D=0.9048$
with all-neighbor $P_D=0.9513$, i.e.\ a $95.1\%$ retention of the upper
reference, and it still outperforms the Sensing-SINR ($0.8360$) and Top-K
Deflection ($0.9034$) baselines while lowering the exchange delay from $19.2$ to
$9.9$ ms. The absolute level is $4.3$ points below the Gaussian abstraction,
which is the price of deleting the $\kappa_\mu=8$ scale; the ordering of the
methods is preserved, confirming that the scheduling behaviour is not an
artefact of that scale, while the per-link information gain is exactly the KLD
\eqref{eq:llr_kld}.

\emph{Finite-blocklength reliability.} With the FBL model
\eqref{eq:fbl_reliability}, the mean report reliability grows from
$\chi\approx0.404$ at blocklength $n=256$ to $\chi\approx0.918$ at $n=512$ and
$\chi\approx1.0$ from $n=1024$ onwards, while the block latency
$n/B_{\mathrm c}$ grows from $0.67$ ms to $22.3$ ms. Correspondingly, $P_D$ rises
from $0.140$ to $0.512$, $0.865$, $0.960$ and $0.987$ at $n=4096$. The FBL sweep
thus exposes the reliability--latency trade-off that the heuristic map
\eqref{eq:heuristic_reliability} cannot express: reliability, payload and
latency are one coupled system, and the useful operating range is
$n\in[1024,4096]$, where reliability has saturated but latency is still
affordable.

\emph{Correlation-aware fusion.} Enabling the observation-covariance fusion
\eqref{eq:correlation_aware_deflection} is essentially neutral under the
corrected model: $P_D$ moves from $0.9481$ to $0.9448$, the mean deflection from
$25.10$ to $24.49$, and the selected-link count from $14.9$ to $15.7$. This is
consistent with the corrected selector already working close to the minimum
link count, so little redundant observation is left for the covariance model to
prune; the main value of the model is that it makes the assumption explicit
rather than silently double-counting correlated pairs.

\emph{Truth vs.\ belief.} Under the belief-mismatch sweep
\eqref{eq:belief_loop}, detection degrades monotonically as the tracker belief
error grows: $P_D=0.9481$ at a perfect tracker falls to $0.3784$ at belief error
$\sigma_p=50~\mathrm m$, to $0.0762$ at $150~\mathrm m$ and to $0.0249$ at
$300~\mathrm m$, with the belief-window capture rate dropping from $1.00$ to
$0.37$, $0.07$ and $0.02$. This quantifies the
predict--schedule--sense--update cost that the earlier ``perturbed-world''
experiments could not capture, and it also identifies the tracking accuracy as
the binding constraint once the reporting budget is resolved.

\emph{Submodularity and the greedy guarantee.} The diminishing-returns audit on
$800$ sampled set pairs reports zero monotonicity violations and zero
submodularity violations, i.e.\ the objective \eqref{eq:task_objective} is
empirically monotone submodular. Its total curvature is $c=0.896$, giving the
guarantee \eqref{eq:greedy_guarantee}
$\frac{1-e^{-c}}{c}=0.660$. On the small-scale instances where an exact search
is tractable, the greedy rule achieves a mean $2.0\%$ gap (median $0.2\%$, max
$25.1\%$) from the same-objective optimum, confirming that the greedy selector
is near-optimal on the very objective it optimises.

\subsection{Interference Coupling and the Sensing-Suppression Budget}
\label{subsec:interference_coupling}

Since a UAV radiates one joint waveform into a shared band, the communication
and the sensing receiver at the same node face the same concurrent transmitters
over the same channel gains \eqref{eq:shared_interference_field}. At the
main-comparison operating point the near-far ratio \eqref{eq:near_far_ratio} is
$\rho_{\mathrm{NF}}=41.3$~dB, the communication interference-to-noise ratio is
$+29.4$~dB, and the direct-path contribution to the sensing interference under
the default $\kappa_{\mathrm{dc}}=40$~dB is $-3.9$~dB. With the guard term
corrected, coupling the two receivers raises the sensing interference by only
$0.287$~dB relative to the decoupled residual floors ($-2.003$ vs.\
$-1.716$~dB), so the structural correction to the sensing side is small. The
coupling is nevertheless not free: it also restores the always-radiated sensing
leakage at the communication receivers, and the illuminator's own direct path at
the sensing receiver, which is why the corrected model is the more conservative
of the two ($P_D=0.9481$ against $0.9629$ for the guard fix alone).

A second, unintended effect turned out to matter more. The SINR guard term, not
the interference model, was dominating the sensing link budget: under the
coupled model with the legacy guard the median sensing SINR is $-22.0$~dB, and
moving the guard below the noise floor raises it to $-10.1$~dB and $P_D$ from
$0.6447$ to $0.9481$. Because the same guard entered the denominator of the
interference-to-noise ratio, it had also deflated that ratio from $-1.7$~dB to
$-16.3$~dB, which is why the sensing interference term was invisible in every
earlier experiment.

Finally, $\kappa_{\mathrm{dc}}$ cannot be chosen freely, and
Table~\ref{tab:suppression_budget} locates the resulting budget. The quantity
that fixes it is the interference-to-noise ratio at the sensing receiver,
$I_j^{\mathrm s,dir}/N_0=+36.1$~dB: the sensing task is viable once the direct
path is cancelled down to the noise floor, and every additional 10~dB of
suppression above that point buys nothing. Keeping everything else fixed, $P_D$
rises from $0.0021$ at $0$~dB to $0.8494$ at $30$~dB and then saturates near
$0.96$ from $40$~dB onwards. Even all-neighbour fusion collapses to $P_D=0.0465$
at zero cancellation: no amount of link selection substitutes for direct-path
suppression. Cooperative sensing therefore requires suppression of the order of
$\rho_{\mathrm{NF}}$, which is the price that sharing the spectrum with the
communication function imposes on the sensing receiver. Reporting this threshold
explicitly, instead of burying it in an unstructured interference floor, is what
makes the scheduling problem an ISAC problem: the illumination that the
communication function requires is the same illumination that limits the sensing
function. Because the absolute $P_D$ at a given $\kappa_{\mathrm{dc}}$ depends on
the soft-statistic model --- the strictly derived LLR model sits $4$--$6$ points
lower at the same operating point --- the suppression budget, not the detection
probability, is the model-robust statement.

The remaining figures and tables in this section use the decoupled interference
floors of the earlier abstraction, for continuity with the frozen reference
results. The coupled instantiation quantified above is the default in the
released code and is available as a configuration preset; it shifts the absolute
detection probabilities while preserving the method ordering and the qualitative
conclusions. In particular, the proposed selector attains $P_D=0.9481$ against
$0.8705$ for sensing-SINR selection and $0.9470$ for Top-K deflection under the
coupled model, versus $0.6506$, $0.6408$ and $0.6593$ respectively under the
decoupled one, while reducing the reporting delay from $688$~ms to $6.9$~ms.
