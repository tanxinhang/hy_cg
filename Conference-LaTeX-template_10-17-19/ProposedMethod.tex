\section{Target-Local Adaptive C2F Selection}
\label{sec:proposed_algorithm}

\subsection{Prediction-Only Fusion Planning}

Let $\widehat{\mathbf p}_q$ be the predicted position supplied by the upstream
tracker. V1 assigns target $q$ to
\begin{equation}
	f_q=\arg\min_{m\in\mathcal M}
	\left\|\widehat{\mathbf p}_q-\mathbf p_m^{\mathrm{UAV}}\right\|_2 .
	\label{eq:nearest_target_fusion}
\end{equation}
The assignment is recomputed from the predicted belief; the true target state
and current-CPI RCS realization are unavailable to the scheduler. Unlike the
previous target-agnostic rule
$\arg\max_m\sum_j R_{jm}$, \eqref{eq:nearest_target_fusion} can map different targets to different fusion
UAVs. It therefore changes the reporting quantities $R_{j f_q}$ and
$\chi_{j f_q}$ before link selection begins.

For target $q$, the resulting feasible candidate set is
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{E}_q=\left\{(i,j):R_{j f_q}\geq R_{\min},\;
	(l_{ijq},k_{ijq})~\text{is DD-valid}\right\}.
	\label{eq:feasible_candidate_set}
\end{equation}
The report generated at UAV $j$ is sent to $f_q$. Under the released serial
finite-blocklength MAC, every selected report occupies $n/B_{\mathrm c}$
seconds and carries 640 bits.

\subsection{Fair Detector-Aligned Marginal}

Let $\mathbf D=(D_1,\ldots,D_Q)$ collect the communication-calibrated fused
deflections. We use the weak-target potential
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
	U(\mathbf D)=&-Q\tau_\alpha\log\!\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}
	\exp[-P_{D,q}(D_q)/\tau_\alpha]\\
	&-\frac{\mu_\alpha}{2D_{\min}}
	\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}[D_{\min}-D_q]^2_+ .
\end{split}
	\label{eq:fair_sensing_utility}
\end{equation}
Its target priority is the gradient
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
	\alpha_q&=Qw_q\frac{\partial P_{D,q}}{\partial D_q}
	+\mu_\alpha\frac{[D_{\min}-D_q]^+}{D_{\min}},\\
	w_q&=\frac{e^{-P_{D,q}/\tau_\alpha}}
	{\sum_r e^{-P_{D,r}/\tau_\alpha}}.
\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:target_priority_compact}
\end{equation}

For candidate $e=(i,j,q)$ and current selection $\mathcal S$, the implemented
greedy replay evaluates
\begin{equation}
	\Delta\mathcal L_e=
	U\!\left(\mathbf D(\mathcal S\cup\{e\})\right)
	-U\!\left(\mathbf D(\mathcal S)\right)
	-\lambda_c\widetilde c_e,
	\label{eq:lagrangian_score_compact}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde c_e$ is the reporting latency in milliseconds. The replay
uses the same post-channel moments and threshold correction as the Monte-Carlo
detector. Starting from $\mathcal S=\varnothing$, it commits the largest
positive marginal while enforcing $|\mathcal S_q|\le L_{\max}$ and
$|\mathcal S|\le L_{\mathrm{tot}}$.

\subsection{Adaptive Two-Stage Refinement}

Computing waveform-derived local DD energy for every feasible triplet is
unnecessary. The coarse stage evaluates candidates using
$\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm c}$ and maintains a dynamic frontier. Promising candidates
are refined with $\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm f}=\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{loc}}$; after each
commit, affected marginals are refreshed and the fine stage replays the exact
detector-aligned rule in \eqref{eq:lagrangian_score_compact}. This is an
adaptive shortlist rather than a fixed one-shot top-$K$ filter. In the V1 main
experiment, the procedure evaluates 117.7 local DD neighborhoods per trial,
versus 509.3 under full refinement, a 76.9\% reduction.

The implemented task objective is
\begin{equation}
	F(\mathcal S)=U\!\left(\mathbf D(\mathcal S)\right)
	-\lambda_c\sum_{e\in\mathcal S}\widetilde c_e .
	\label{eq:task_objective}
\end{equation}
The cost term can make $F$ non-monotone, while the soft minimum couples targets
and the constraints combine per-target and total caps. We therefore make no
universal submodular approximation claim. V1 is an architectural extension of
the existing selector: its principal new variable is $f_q$ in
\eqref{eq:nearest_target_fusion}, not a new greedy score.
