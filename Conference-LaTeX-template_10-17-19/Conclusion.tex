\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented target-local fusion V1 for prior-assisted multi-UAV
OTFS-ISAC sensing. The outer planner assigns each target to the UAV nearest its
predicted position, while the existing communication-calibrated adaptive C2F
selector performs detector-aligned greedy replay. The formulation therefore
changes where reports meet without changing the inner marginal-gain objective,
packet size, blocklength, or resource caps.

In 1000 paired trials, V1 achieved $P_D=0.9734$ and worst-target
$P_D=0.968$ with 11.993 reports, 7.676~kbit, and 12.793~ms of serial
reporting. At identical resources, its paired gains over exact-marginal greedy
and Sensing-SINR were 0.0067 and 0.0085, respectively. A controlled fusion-rule
ablation showed that target-local planning disperses targets across fusion UAVs
and exposes shorter, more reliable reporting edges to the inner selector. The
evidence therefore supports a joint architectural conclusion---target-related
fusion planning plus adaptive two-stage selection---rather than attributing the
full improvement to a new greedy criterion.

V1 is a stable nominal version, not a universal endpoint. Large prediction
errors, strict rate thresholds, and sparse deployments remain its principal
boundaries. Future work will validate the main ablations with multiple seeds and
1000-trial paired tests, model obstruction and correlated reports, compare the
1536- and 2048-use operating points, and turn the observed conflict-slot
reduction into an interference- and finite-blocklength-aware parallel scheduler.
