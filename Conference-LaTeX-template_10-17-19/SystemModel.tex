\section{System Model}

\label{sec:system_model}

We consider a sensing-centric multi-UAV OTFS-ISAC network, as shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:system_framework}(a). The UAV and target sets are denoted by
$\mathcal M=\{1,\ldots,M\}$ and $\mathcal Q=\{1,\ldots,Q\}$, respectively.
UAV $i$ and target $q$ have position--velocity pairs
$(\mathbf p_i,\mathbf v_i)$ and $(\mathbf p_q,\mathbf v_q)$. A cooperative
candidate is indexed by the target-dependent triplet $(i,j,q)$, where UAV $i$
transmits an OTFS-ISAC probing signal, target $q$ reflects it, and UAV $j$
receives the bistatic echo. The inter-UAV reporting topology is represented by
a directed graph $\mathcal G=(\mathcal M,\mathcal E)$, where
$(i,j)\in\mathcal E$ if $\|\mathbf p_i-\mathbf p_j\|\le d_{\max}$ and
$i\ne j$.

We explicitly separate the \emph{sensing} topology from the
\emph{reporting} topology. The sensing chain is
$i\rightarrow q\rightarrow j$: UAV $j$ receives the echo and therefore
\emph{produces} the soft statistic. The statistic is then reported over the
link $j\rightarrow f_q$ to a target-specific fusion UAV $f_q\in\mathcal M$,
which is the single node where all soft statistics about target $q$ meet.
Accordingly, a sensing-reporting candidate is the quadruple
$(i,j,q;f_q)$, and the communication quantities that matter---reliability
$\chi_{j f_q}$, rate $R_{j f_q}$ and delay $T_{j f_q}$---are properties of the
reporting leg $(j,f_q)$ alone, with no dependence on the illuminator $i$.
Every ordered pair $i\ne j$ is a potential bistatic sensing pair; communication
range constrains only the remote reporting leg. If $j=f_q$, the fusion UAV
retains its locally produced statistic without transmitting a packet.
This separation ensures that the fused statistic is physically well
defined: with a naive $j\rightarrow i$ reporting rule, three selected links
$(i_1,j_1,q)$, $(i_2,j_2,q)$, $(i_3,j_3,q)$ would deliver their statistics to
three different UAVs while the fusion step implicitly assumed they had already
met. We use a geometry-driven soft-information abstraction rather than a full
waveform-level OTFS receiver model. The OTFS structure enters the scheduling
problem through DD-bin validity, fractional DD energy loss, same-bin collision,
and local DD-grid refinement. These DD-domain quantities determine whether a
target-dependent triplet provides useful soft information after reporting and
fusion.

\subsection{Communication and Reporting Model}
\label{subsec:comm_model}

The transmit power of UAV $i$ is split into sensing and communication parts,
i.e.,
\begin{equation}
	P_i^{\mathrm s}=\rho P_i,\qquad
	P_i^{\mathrm c}=(1-\rho)P_i .
	\label{eq:power_split}
\end{equation}
For a directed inter-UAV link $(a,b)$, the communication-channel power gain is
\begin{equation}
	g_{ab}^{\mathrm c}
	=
	\left(\frac{\lambda}{4\pi}\right)^2
	d_{ab}^{-\alpha_{\mathrm c}}
	\zeta_{ab}|h_{ab}^{\mathrm c}|^2,
	\qquad
	d_{ab}=\|\mathbf p_a-\mathbf p_b\|.
	\label{eq:comm_channel_gain}
\end{equation}
Here $\lambda$, $\alpha_{\mathrm c}$, $\zeta_{ab}$, and $h_{ab}^{\mathrm c}$
denote the carrier wavelength, path-loss exponent, shadowing, and small-scale
fading, respectively.

The conference release uses orthogonal inter-UAV reporting. The communication
SINR from UAV $i$ to UAV $j$ is therefore
\begin{equation}
	\gamma_{ij}^{\mathrm c}
	=
	\frac{P_i^{\mathrm c}g_{ij}^{\mathrm c}}
	{N_0+
		\epsilon_{\mathrm s}\!\sum_{k\in\mathcal M\setminus\{i,j\}}
		P_k^{\mathrm s}g_{kj}^{\mathrm c}
		+\epsilon_{\mathrm d}P_i^{\mathrm s}g_{ij}^{\mathrm c}} .
	\label{eq:comm_sinr}
\end{equation}
The coefficients $\epsilon_{\mathrm s}$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm d}$ represent
neighbor-induced sensing-to-communication leakage and intra-link leakage,
respectively. Other report payloads do not enter \eqref{eq:comm_sinr}, because
strictly serial reports cannot interfere with one another.

\emph{Shared-spectrum coupling with an orthogonal reporting phase.} All UAVs
continuously radiate their sensing components over the same propagation field.
Soft reports are then exchanged in orthogonal slots. Thus, the communication
receiver sees sensing-waveform leakage, while the sensing receiver is limited by
residual direct paths from the concurrently radiated sensing waveforms:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		I_{ij}^{\mathrm c}
		&=
		\epsilon_{\mathrm s}
		\sum_{k\in\mathcal M\setminus\{i,j\}}
		P_k^{\mathrm s}g_{kj}^{\mathrm c}, \\[2pt]
		I_{j}^{\mathrm s}
		&=
		\kappa_{\mathrm{self}}P_j
		+
		\kappa_{\mathrm{dc}}
		\sum_{k\in\mathcal M\setminus\{j\}}
		P_k^{\mathrm s}g_{kj}^{\mathrm c}.
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:shared_interference_field}
\end{equation}
The phase separation removes circular payload interference without removing
ISAC coupling: sensing waveforms still leak into the report receiver and their
residual direct paths limit sensing. Here $\kappa_{\mathrm{dc}}$ denotes the
residual after reconstructing and cancelling the known illuminator waveforms.

With communication bandwidth $B_{\mathrm c}=N\Delta f$, the reporting rate is
\begin{equation}
	R_{ij}=B_{\mathrm c}\log_2(1+\gamma_{ij}^{\mathrm c}),
	\label{eq:rate_delay}
\end{equation}
and a remote reporting link is feasible when $R_{ij}\ge R_{\min}$ and
$\chi_{ij}\ge\chi_{\min}$. For local evidence we define
$T_{j f_q}=B_{j f_q}=0$ and $\chi_{j f_q}=1$ when $j=f_q$.

\emph{Finite-blocklength reliability.} A soft sensing report is a short control
packet. The canonical simulation uses the normal approximation
\begin{equation}
	\epsilon_{ij}
	\approx
	\mathsf Q
	\left(
	\frac{
		C(\gamma_{ij}^{\mathrm c})-\frac{k}{n}
	}{
		\sqrt{V(\gamma_{ij}^{\mathrm c})/n}
	}
	\right),
	\qquad
	\chi_{ij}=1-\epsilon_{ij},
	\label{eq:fbl_reliability}
\end{equation}
where $C(\gamma)=\log_2(1+\gamma)$,
$V(\gamma)=\left(1-(1+\gamma)^{-2}\right)(\log_2 e)^2$, $k=B_q$ is the payload
in bits, and $n$ is the blocklength. Reliability, payload, and latency are then
coupled through $\chi_{ij}=1-\epsilon_{ij}$ and $T_{ij}=n/B_{\mathrm c}$.
The heuristic reliability map is retained only for legacy-result reproduction.

\subsection{DD-Domain Bistatic Sensing Model}
\label{subsec:dd_sensing_model}

For triplet $(i,j,q)$, define
$d_{iq}=\|\mathbf p_q-\mathbf p_i\|$,
$d_{jq}=\|\mathbf p_q-\mathbf p_j\|$,
$\mathbf u_{iq}=(\mathbf p_q-\mathbf p_i)/d_{iq}$, and
$\mathbf u_{jq}=(\mathbf p_q-\mathbf p_j)/d_{jq}$. The bistatic delay and
Doppler shift are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
	\tau_{ijq}&=\frac{d_{iq}+d_{jq}}{c},\\
	\nu_{ijq}&=
	\frac{(\mathbf v_q-\mathbf v_i)^T\mathbf u_{iq}}
	{\lambda}
	+\frac{(\mathbf v_q-\mathbf v_j)^T\mathbf u_{jq}}
	{\lambda}.
\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:bistatic_delay_doppler}
\end{equation}

The corresponding DD-grid indices are
\begin{equation}
	l_{ijq}=\operatorname{round}(\tau_{ijq}L\Delta f),\qquad
	k_{ijq}=\operatorname{round}(\nu_{ijq}NT).
	\label{eq:dd_grid_mapping}
\end{equation}
A triplet is DD-valid if $0\le l_{ijq}<L$ and
$-N/2\le k_{ijq}<N/2$. The fractional DD offsets are
\begin{equation}
	\varepsilon_{ijq}^{\tau}=\tau_{ijq}L\Delta f-l_{ijq},\qquad
	\varepsilon_{ijq}^{\nu}=\nu_{ijq}NT-k_{ijq}.
	\label{eq:dd_fractional_offsets}
\end{equation}

Using the normalized sinc function, the coarse DD-domain gain is
\begin{equation}
	\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm c}
	=
	\operatorname{sinc}^2(\varepsilon_{ijq}^{\tau})
	\operatorname{sinc}^2(\varepsilon_{ijq}^{\nu}).
	\label{eq:coarse_dd_gain}
\end{equation}
For C2F DD refinement, a local $(2W+1)\times(2W+1)$ DD neighborhood provides
the waveform-derived recoverable energy $\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{loc}}$. The refined
gain is
\begin{equation}
	\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm f}
	=
	\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{loc}}.
	\label{eq:fine_dd_gain}
\end{equation}
Thus, C2F changes computational effort rather than introducing a hand-set
sensing-gain interpolation.

If multiple DD-valid targets observed by the same UAV pair fall into the same
DD bin, we use the collision penalty
\begin{equation}
	\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{col}}
	=
	\frac{1}{n_{ijq}^{\alpha_{\mathrm{col}}}},
	\label{eq:dd_collision_penalty}
\end{equation}
where $n_{ijq}$ is the number of targets sharing bin $(l_{ijq},k_{ijq})$.

The bistatic target gain is
\begin{equation}
	g_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}
	=
	\frac{\lambda^2\sigma_q}{(4\pi)^3d_{iq}^2d_{jq}^2},
	\label{eq:bistatic_target_gain}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_q$ denotes the target RCS. Combining bistatic gain, processing
gain, DD-domain gain, and collision penalty gives
\begin{equation}
	\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}
	=
	\frac{
		\widetilde P_i^{\mathrm s}
		g_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}
		G_{\mathrm p}
		\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{DD}}
		\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{col}}
	}
	{N_0+I_{j}^{\mathrm s}},
	\qquad
	\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{DD}}
	\in
	\{\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm c},\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm f}\}.
	\label{eq:sensing_sinr}
\end{equation}
In the sensing-only power model, $\widetilde P_i^{\mathrm s}=P_i^{\mathrm s}$
and $G_{\mathrm p}=NL$. Both receivers use the shared propagation field in
\eqref{eq:shared_interference_field}, with receiver-specific suppression. The
illuminator's direct path remains in $I_j^{\mathrm s}$, which makes
\eqref{eq:sensing_sinr} near--far limited rather than interference free.

\subsection{Soft-Information Fusion Model}
\label{subsec:soft_fusion_model}

Each feasible triplet produces a waveform-derived local log-likelihood ratio
(LLR). After OTFS matched filtering, the delay-Doppler bin for target $q$ on link
$i\rightarrow q\rightarrow j$ is a single complex sample under an independent
fast-fluctuation, Swerling-II-like model. The normalised bin energy
$x=|z|^2/\sigma_n^2$ is therefore
exponential, $x\sim\mathsf{Exp}(1)$ under $\mathcal H_0$ and
$x\sim\mathsf{Exp}(1+\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s})$ under $\mathcal H_1$. The local
log-likelihood ratio, centred under $\mathcal H_0$, is
\begin{equation}
	\ell_{ijq}
	=
	\frac{\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}}{1+\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}}
	\left(x-L\right),
	\label{eq:local_llr}
\end{equation}
where $L$ independent frames are incoherently integrated in one CPI, so their
summed energy is Gamma distributed. Its
moments are
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\delta_{ijq}
		&= \mathbb E_1[\ell]-\mathbb E_0[\ell]
		= \frac{L(\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s})^2}{1+\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}}, \\[2pt]
		\sigma_{ij,0}^2
		&= \mathrm{Var}_0[\ell]
		= \frac{L(\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s})^2}{(1+\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s})^2},
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:llr_moments}
\end{equation}
The LLR carries no free scale parameter, and its single-link deflection
$\delta_{ijq}^2/\sigma_{ij,0}^2=L(\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s})^2$ follows directly
from this independent-look model.

Communication-error calibration acts on the soft statistic through the
reporting leg $j\rightarrow f_q$:
\begin{equation}
	\delta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{eff}}=\chi_{j f_q}\delta_{ijq},\qquad
	\bar{\sigma}_{ij,0}^2
	=
	\chi_{j f_q}\sigma_{ij,0}^2
	+
	(1-\chi_{j f_q})\sigma_{\mathrm{err}}^2,
	\label{eq:comm_error_calibration}
\end{equation}
where $\chi_{j f_q}$ is the success probability \eqref{eq:fbl_reliability} of
the report $j\rightarrow f_q$. The same post-channel moments generate the
selection metric, the detection threshold, and the Monte Carlo samples.

For selected set $\mathcal S_q$, the released V1 model assumes conditionally
independent local statistics, giving
\begin{equation}
	D_q(\mathcal S_q)
	=
	\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal S_q}
	\frac{(\delta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{eff}})^2}{\bar\sigma_{ij,0}^2}.
	\label{eq:independent_deflection}
\end{equation}
Modeling correlated reports is left to future work.

Using the Gaussian deflection approximation, the detection probability is
\begin{equation}
	P_{D,q}
	\approx
	\mathsf Q
	\left(
	\mathsf Q^{-1}(P_{\mathrm{FA}})
	-
	\sqrt{D_q(\mathcal S_q)}
	\right),
	\label{eq:pd_approx}
\end{equation}
where $\mathsf Q(\cdot)$ denotes the Gaussian $Q$-function. Because the local
LLR has a positively skewed centred-Gamma null distribution, the Monte Carlo
detector calibrates the threshold with the Cornish--Fisher correction
$z_{\mathrm{CF}}=z+(\gamma_{1,0}/6)(z^2-1)$, where $\gamma_{1,0}$ is computed
from the same post-report moments used by fusion.

\subsection{Truth, Belief and the Scheduling Loop}
\label{subsec:truth_belief}

We study prior-assisted confirmation or re-detection after a coarse acquisition
stage; blind first acquisition is outside the present scope. The scheduler
therefore receives an upstream predicted belief but cannot access the true target
state. We carry two states side by side. The \emph{truth}
$(\mathbf p_{q,t},\mathbf v_{q,t})$ drives echo
generation, the true delay-Doppler, the true sensing gain and the detector;
the \emph{belief} $b_{q,t}=\mathcal N(\hat{\mathbf x}_{q,t},\mathbf P_{q,t})$
drives link selection and the DD search window. The intended tracking loop is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\text{predict}
		\;\rightarrow\;
		\text{schedule}
		\;\rightarrow\;
		\text{sense}
		\;\rightarrow\;
		\text{update}, \\[2pt]
		&\hat{\mathbf x}_{t|t-1}
		=
		\mathbf F\hat{\mathbf x}_{t-1|t-1},\\[2pt]
		&\mathbf P_{t|t-1}
		=
		\mathbf F\mathbf P_{t-1|t-1}\mathbf F^T+\mathbf Q .
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:belief_loop}
\end{equation}
The simulator instantiates this belief as an upstream Gaussian snapshot rather
than implementing the posterior update. Its covariance is propagated through
the bistatic DD Jacobian to form a three-sigma gate. Scheduling uses mean RCS;
the fast look-to-look fluctuation is marginalized by \eqref{eq:local_llr}.
Thus, neither target-state truth nor a realized scattering coefficient enters
scheduling.
