\section{System Model}

\label{sec:system_model}

We consider a sensing-centric multi-UAV OTFS-ISAC network, as shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:system_framework}(a). The UAV and target sets are denoted by
$\mathcal M=\{1,\ldots,M\}$ and $\mathcal Q=\{1,\ldots,Q\}$, respectively.
UAV $i$ and target $q$ have position--velocity pairs
$(\mathbf p_i,\mathbf v_i)$ and $(\mathbf p_q,\mathbf v_q)$. A cooperative
candidate is indexed by the target-dependent triplet $(i,j,q)$, where UAV $i$
transmits an OTFS-ISAC probing signal, target $q$ reflects it, and UAV $j$
receives the bistatic echo. The inter-UAV reporting topology is represented by
a directed graph $\mathcal G=(\mathcal M,\mathcal E)$, where
$(i,j)\in\mathcal E$ if $\|\mathbf p_i-\mathbf p_j\|\le d_{\max}$ and
$i\ne j$.

We explicitly separate the \emph{sensing} topology from the
\emph{reporting} topology. The sensing chain is
$i\rightarrow q\rightarrow j$: UAV $j$ receives the echo and therefore
\emph{produces} the soft statistic. The statistic is then reported over the
link $j\rightarrow f_q$ to a target-specific fusion UAV $f_q\in\mathcal M$,
which is the single node where all soft statistics about target $q$ meet.
Accordingly, a sensing-reporting candidate is the quadruple
$(i,j,q;f_q)$, and the communication quantities that matter---reliability
$\chi_{j f_q}$, rate $R_{j f_q}$ and delay $T_{j f_q}$---are properties of the
reporting leg $(j,f_q)$ alone, with no dependence on the illuminator $i$.
This decoupling is what guarantees the fused statistic is physically well
defined: with a naive $j\rightarrow i$ reporting rule, three selected links
$(i_1,j_1,q)$, $(i_2,j_2,q)$, $(i_3,j_3,q)$ would deliver their statistics to
three different UAVs while the fusion step implicitly assumed they had already
met. We use a geometry-driven soft-information abstraction rather than a full
waveform-level OTFS receiver model. The OTFS structure enters the scheduling
problem through DD-bin validity, fractional DD energy loss, same-bin collision,
and local DD-grid refinement. These DD-domain quantities determine whether a
target-dependent triplet provides useful soft information after reporting and
fusion.

\subsection{Communication and Reporting Model}
\label{subsec:comm_model}

The transmit power of UAV $i$ is split into sensing and communication parts,
i.e.,
\begin{equation}
	P_i^{\mathrm s}=\rho P_i,\qquad
	P_i^{\mathrm c}=(1-\rho)P_i .
	\label{eq:power_split}
\end{equation}
For a directed inter-UAV link $(a,b)$, the communication-channel power gain is
\begin{equation}
	g_{ab}^{\mathrm c}
	=
	\left(\frac{\lambda}{4\pi}\right)^2
	d_{ab}^{-\alpha_{\mathrm c}}
	\zeta_{ab}|h_{ab}^{\mathrm c}|^2,
	\qquad
	d_{ab}=\|\mathbf p_a-\mathbf p_b\|.
	\label{eq:comm_channel_gain}
\end{equation}
Here $\lambda$, $\alpha_{\mathrm c}$, $\zeta_{ab}$, and $h_{ab}^{\mathrm c}$
denote the carrier wavelength, path-loss exponent, shadowing, and small-scale
fading, respectively.

The communication SINR from UAV $i$ to UAV $j$ is modeled as
\begin{equation}
	\gamma_{ij}^{\mathrm c}
	=
	\frac{P_i^{\mathrm c}g_{ij}^{\mathrm c}}
	{N_0+
		\sum_{k\in\mathcal M\setminus\{i,j\}}
		\left(P_k^{\mathrm c}+\epsilon_{\mathrm s}P_k^{\mathrm s}\right)
		g_{kj}^{\mathrm c}
		+\epsilon_{\mathrm d}P_i^{\mathrm s}g_{ij}^{\mathrm c}} .
	\label{eq:comm_sinr}
\end{equation}
The coefficients $\epsilon_{\mathrm s}$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm d}$ represent
neighbor-induced sensing-to-communication leakage and intra-link leakage from
the sensing component of UAV $i$ to the communication receiver, respectively.

\emph{One interference field, two receivers.} Because each UAV radiates a
\emph{single} joint waveform into a shared band, the very same concurrent
transmitters reach the communication and the sensing receiver through the very
same channel \eqref{eq:comm_channel_gain}. Let
$\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal M$ be the set of UAVs that actually transmit a
report payload; the radiated ISAC power of UAV $k$ is then
\begin{equation}
	P_k^{\mathrm{tx}}
	=
	P_k^{\mathrm s}
	+
	\mathbb 1\{k\in\mathcal A\}\,P_k^{\mathrm c},
	\label{eq:radiated_power}
\end{equation}
since the sensing waveform is radiated continuously, and the two receivers at
node $j$ observe
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		I_{j}^{\mathrm c}
		&=
		\sum_{k\in\mathcal M\setminus\{j\}}
		\left(
			\mathbb 1\{k\in\mathcal A\}P_k^{\mathrm c}
			+
			\epsilon_{\mathrm s}P_k^{\mathrm s}
		\right)
		g_{kj}^{\mathrm c}, \\[2pt]
		I_{j}^{\mathrm s}
		&=
		\kappa_{\mathrm{self}}P_j
		+
		\kappa_{\mathrm{dc}}
		\sum_{k\in\mathcal M\setminus\{j\}}
		P_k^{\mathrm{tx}}g_{kj}^{\mathrm c}.
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:shared_interference_field}
\end{equation}
Giving the communication receiver a full interference term while treating the
sensing interference as an unstructured floor is not an ISAC model: it removes
precisely the coupling that makes the two functions worth co-designing. Setting
$\mathcal A=\mathcal M$ recovers the full-concurrent form
\eqref{eq:comm_sinr}; the reported results use the scheduled form, in which only
UAVs that actually report contribute their payload, whereas the sensing
waveform---being what produces the echoes in the first place---is always
radiated. Two structural properties are worth stating. First, the illuminator $i$ \emph{is}
an interferer on the sensing side, because its direct path competes with its own
target echo; on the communication side the same UAV is the wanted source. This
is a difference in the role a node plays, not in the physics. Second,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{dc}}$ is not a tuning floor but the \emph{direct-path
cancellation} the sensing receiver achieves: cooperative ISAC knows every
illuminator's waveform, so the deterministic direct path can be reconstructed
and subtracted, and only the channel-estimation residual remains.

The suppression $\kappa_{\mathrm{dc}}$ cannot be chosen freely, because the
interference and the useful signal do not scale alike. By
\eqref{eq:bistatic_target_gain} the echo suffers a fourth-power path loss while
the direct path suffers a second-power one, so their ratio
\begin{equation}
	\rho_{\mathrm{NF}}
	=
	\frac{
		\sum_{k\in\mathcal M\setminus\{j\}}P_k^{\mathrm{tx}}g_{kj}^{\mathrm c}
	}{
		\widetilde P_i^{\mathrm s}g_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}G_{\mathrm p}
	}
	\label{eq:near_far_ratio}
\end{equation}
grows as $d^2$ and is $\rho_{\mathrm{NF}}\approx 40$~dB for the geometry of
Section~\ref{sec:simulation_results}. The sensing task therefore survives only if
$\kappa_{\mathrm{dc}}\lesssim\rho_{\mathrm{NF}}^{-1}$: the required suppression
budget is itself the price that sharing the spectrum with the communication
function imposes on the sensing receiver. We accordingly treat
$\kappa_{\mathrm{dc}}$ as a first-class system parameter and sweep it, rather
than asserting a value.

With communication bandwidth $B_{\mathrm c}=N\Delta f$, the reporting rate and
exchange delay are
\begin{equation}
	R_{ij}=B_{\mathrm c}\log_2(1+\gamma_{ij}^{\mathrm c}),\qquad
	c_{ijq}=\frac{B_q}{R_{ij}},
	\label{eq:rate_delay}
\end{equation}
where $B_q=K_{\mathrm{pkt}}b_{\mathrm d}$ is the soft-information payload size.
A reporting link is feasible when $R_{ij}\ge R_{\min}$.

\emph{Finite-blocklength reliability.} A soft sensing report is a short control
packet. The default simulation uses the compact abstraction
\begin{equation}
	\chi_{ij}^{\mathrm{heur}}
	=
	\frac{\gamma_{ij}^{\mathrm c}}
	{\gamma_{ij}^{\mathrm c}+\gamma_{\mathrm{req}}},
	\qquad
	\gamma_{\mathrm{req}}=2^{R_{\min}/B_{\mathrm c}}-1,
	\label{eq:heuristic_reliability}
\end{equation}
which is a smooth monotone map but carries no probabilistic meaning. Its
strictly probabilistic counterpart is the finite-blocklength packet success
probability: the packet error probability is, by the normal approximation,
\begin{equation}
	\epsilon_{ij}
	\approx
	\mathsf Q
	\left(
	\frac{
		C(\gamma_{ij}^{\mathrm c})-\frac{k}{n}
	}{
		\sqrt{V(\gamma_{ij}^{\mathrm c})/n}
	}
	\right),
	\qquad
	\chi_{ij}=1-\epsilon_{ij},
	\label{eq:fbl_reliability}
\end{equation}
where $C(\gamma)=\log_2(1+\gamma)$,
$V(\gamma)=\left(1-(1+\gamma)^{-2}\right)(\log_2 e)^2$, $k=B_q$ is the payload
in bits and $n$ is the blocklength in channel uses. The report therefore has a
genuine \emph{packet success probability} $\chi_{ij}$, and reliability, payload
and latency become one coupled system: $k$ enters the error exponent, while $n$
enters both the error exponent and the block latency $T_{ij}=n/B_{\mathrm c}$.

\subsection{DD-Domain Bistatic Sensing Model}
\label{subsec:dd_sensing_model}

For triplet $(i,j,q)$, define
$d_{iq}=\|\mathbf p_q-\mathbf p_i\|$,
$d_{jq}=\|\mathbf p_q-\mathbf p_j\|$,
$\mathbf u_{iq}=(\mathbf p_q-\mathbf p_i)/d_{iq}$, and
$\mathbf u_{jq}=(\mathbf p_q-\mathbf p_j)/d_{jq}$. The bistatic delay and
Doppler shift are
\begin{equation}
	\tau_{ijq}=\frac{d_{iq}+d_{jq}}{c},\qquad
	\nu_{ijq}
	=
	\frac{
		(\mathbf v_q-\mathbf v_i)^T\mathbf u_{iq}
		+
		(\mathbf v_q-\mathbf v_j)^T\mathbf u_{jq}}
	{\lambda}.
	\label{eq:bistatic_delay_doppler}
\end{equation}

The corresponding DD-grid indices are
\begin{equation}
	l_{ijq}=\operatorname{round}(\tau_{ijq}L\Delta f),\qquad
	k_{ijq}=\operatorname{round}(\nu_{ijq}NT).
	\label{eq:dd_grid_mapping}
\end{equation}
A triplet is DD-valid if $0\le l_{ijq}<L$ and
$-N/2\le k_{ijq}<N/2$. The fractional DD offsets are
\begin{equation}
	\varepsilon_{ijq}^{\tau}=\tau_{ijq}L\Delta f-l_{ijq},\qquad
	\varepsilon_{ijq}^{\nu}=\nu_{ijq}NT-k_{ijq}.
	\label{eq:dd_fractional_offsets}
\end{equation}

Using the normalized sinc function, the coarse DD-domain gain is
\begin{equation}
	\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm c}
	=
	\operatorname{sinc}^2(\varepsilon_{ijq}^{\tau})
	\operatorname{sinc}^2(\varepsilon_{ijq}^{\nu}).
	\label{eq:coarse_dd_gain}
\end{equation}
For C2F DD refinement, a local $(2W+1)\times(2W+1)$ DD neighborhood provides
the recoverable energy $\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{loc}}$, and the refined gain is
\begin{equation}
	\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm f}
	=
	\min\!\left\{
	1,\,
	\max\!\left[
	\eta_{\min},
	\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm c}
	+
	\kappa_{\mathrm{dd}}
	(\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{loc}}-\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm c})
	\right]
	\right\}.
	\label{eq:fine_dd_gain}
\end{equation}
Here $\kappa_{\mathrm{dd}}\in[0,1]$ controls the refinement strength and
$\eta_{\min}$ is the retained-energy floor.

If multiple DD-valid targets observed by the same UAV pair fall into the same
DD bin, we use the collision penalty
\begin{equation}
	\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{col}}
	=
	\frac{1}{n_{ijq}^{\alpha_{\mathrm{col}}}},
	\label{eq:dd_collision_penalty}
\end{equation}
where $n_{ijq}$ is the number of targets sharing bin $(l_{ijq},k_{ijq})$.

The bistatic target gain is
\begin{equation}
	g_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}
	=
	\frac{\lambda^2\sigma_q}{(4\pi)^3d_{iq}^2d_{jq}^2},
	\label{eq:bistatic_target_gain}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_q$ denotes the target RCS. Combining bistatic gain, processing
gain, DD-domain gain, and collision penalty gives
\begin{equation}
	\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}
	=
	\frac{
		\widetilde P_i^{\mathrm s}
		g_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}
		G_{\mathrm p}
		\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{DD}}
		\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{col}}
	}
	{N_0+I_{j}^{\mathrm s}},
	\qquad
	\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{DD}}
	\in
	\{\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm c},\eta_{ijq}^{\mathrm f}\}.
	\label{eq:sensing_sinr}
\end{equation}
In the default sensing-only mode, $\widetilde P_i^{\mathrm s}=P_i^{\mathrm s}$,
and $G_{\mathrm p}=NL$. The interference term is the \emph{same field}
\eqref{eq:shared_interference_field} that the communication receiver at $j$
sees, attenuated only by the direct-path cancellation $\kappa_{\mathrm{dc}}$
that the sensing receiver achieves; the illuminator's own direct path is part
of it, which is what makes \eqref{eq:sensing_sinr} a genuine near-far
limited problem rather than an interference-free one.

\subsection{Soft-Information Fusion Model}
\label{subsec:soft_fusion_model}

Each feasible triplet produces a scalar soft statistic $s_{ijq}$. The default
simulation uses the compact Gaussian abstraction
\begin{equation}
	\mu_{ijq}
	=
	\kappa_\mu\log(1+\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}),
	\qquad
	\sigma_{ij,0}^2
	=
	\sigma_0^2\left(1+\xi I_{j}^{\mathrm s}/N_0\right),
	\label{eq:gaussian_soft_stat}
\end{equation}
with a hand-set scale $\kappa_\mu$; this encodes the tautology ``a higher
sensing SINR gives a larger soft mean'' and nothing more.

\emph{Strictly derived local LLR.} Because $\kappa_\mu$ carries no physical
meaning, we also provide the strictly derived statistic. After OTFS matched
filtering, the content of the delay-Doppler bin carrying target $q$ on link
$i\rightarrow q\rightarrow j$ is a single complex sample with a Swerling-I
fluctuation. The normalised bin energy $x=|z|^2/\sigma_n^2$ is therefore
exponential, $x\sim\mathsf{Exp}(1)$ under $\mathcal H_0$ and
$x\sim\mathsf{Exp}(1+\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s})$ under $\mathcal H_1$. The local
log-likelihood ratio, centred under $\mathcal H_0$, is
\begin{equation}
	\ell_{ijq}
	=
	\frac{\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}}{1+\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}}
	\left(x-L\right),
	\label{eq:local_llr}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the number of OTFS frames incoherently integrated in one CPI. Its
moments are
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\delta_{ijq}
		&= \mathbb E_1[\ell]-\mathbb E_0[\ell]
		= \frac{L(\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s})^2}{1+\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}}, \\[2pt]
		\sigma_{ij,0}^2
		&= \mathrm{Var}_0[\ell]
		= \frac{L(\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s})^2}{(1+\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s})^2},
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:llr_moments}
\end{equation}
and the per-link information gain is a Kullback-Leibler divergence
\begin{equation}
	J_{ijq}
	=
	D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_1\|p_0)
	=
	L\left(\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s}-\ln(1+\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s})\right).
	\label{eq:llr_kld}
\end{equation}
This strictly derived statistic carries no free scale parameter, and its
single-link deflection
$\delta_{ijq}^2/\sigma_{ij,0}^2=L(\gamma_{ijq}^{\mathrm s})^2$ is Swerling's
classical result. The Gaussian abstraction \eqref{eq:gaussian_soft_stat} is
retained as the default because it isolates the scheduling behaviour from the
detector-level operating point; the LLR model is validated in
Section~\ref{sec:simulation_results}.

Communication-error calibration acts on the soft statistic through the
reporting leg $j\rightarrow f_q$:
\begin{equation}
	\delta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{eff}}=\chi_{j f_q}\delta_{ijq},\qquad
	\bar{\sigma}_{ij,0}^2
	=
	\chi_{j f_q}\sigma_{ij,0}^2
	+
	(1-\chi_{j f_q})\sigma_{\mathrm{err}}^2,
	\label{eq:comm_error_calibration}
\end{equation}
where $\chi_{j f_q}$ is the success probability \eqref{eq:fbl_reliability} of
the report $j\rightarrow f_q$.

For selected set $\mathcal S_q$, let
$\boldsymbol\delta_q=(\delta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{eff}})_{(i,j)\in\mathcal S_q}$ and
let $\mathbf\Sigma_q$ be the covariance of the stacked local statistics. The
default assumes the local statistics are independent, giving
\begin{equation}
	D_q(\mathcal S_q)
	=
	\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal S_q}
	\frac{(\delta_{ijq}^{\mathrm{eff}})^2}{\bar\sigma_{ij,0}^2}.
	\label{eq:independent_deflection}
\end{equation}
In practice the local statistics are correlated (shared transmitters, shared
receivers, the shared Swerling fluctuation of target $q$ and possibly
neighbouring DD support). The correlation-aware fusion generalises to
\begin{equation}
	D_q(\mathcal S_q)
	=
	\boldsymbol\delta_q^T \mathbf\Sigma_q^{-1} \boldsymbol\delta_q,
	\qquad
	\mathbf w_q^\star
	\propto
	\mathbf\Sigma_q^{-1}\boldsymbol\delta_q,
	\label{eq:correlation_aware_deflection}
\end{equation}
which collapses to \eqref{eq:independent_deflection} when
$\mathbf\Sigma_q$ is diagonal. Correlated, redundant UAV observations are thus
down-weighted by the mathematics rather than by a hand-set diversity factor.

Approximating $F_q$ as Gaussian, the detection probability is
\begin{equation}
	P_{D,q}
	\approx
	\mathsf Q
	\left(
	\mathsf Q^{-1}(P_{\mathrm{FA}})
	-
	\sqrt{D_q(\mathcal S_q)}
	\right),
	\label{eq:pd_approx}
\end{equation}
where $\mathsf Q(\cdot)$ denotes the Gaussian $Q$-function.

\subsection{Truth, Belief and the Scheduling Loop}
\label{subsec:truth_belief}

Detection has not happened when the scheduler selects links, so the scheduler
cannot be given the true target state. We therefore carry two states side by
side. The \emph{truth} $(\mathbf p_{q,t},\mathbf v_{q,t})$ drives echo
generation, the true delay-Doppler, the true sensing gain and the detector;
the \emph{belief} $b_{q,t}=\mathcal N(\hat{\mathbf x}_{q,t},\mathbf P_{q,t})$
drives link selection, the DD search window and the resource budget. The loop
is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\text{predict}
		\;\rightarrow\;
		\text{schedule}
		\;\rightarrow\;
		\text{sense}
		\;\rightarrow\;
		\text{update}, \\[2pt]
		&\hat{\mathbf x}_{t|t-1}
		=
		\mathbf F\hat{\mathbf x}_{t-1|t-1},\\[2pt]
		&\mathbf P_{t|t-1}
		=
		\mathbf F\mathbf P_{t-1|t-1}\mathbf F^T+\mathbf Q .
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:belief_loop}
\end{equation}
A selected link delivers target evidence only when the belief-guided search
window captures the true delay-Doppler bin; a mismatched belief therefore
degrades detection exactly as a reviewer would expect, and this is quantified
in Section~\ref{sec:simulation_results} through a belief-mismatch sweep.
